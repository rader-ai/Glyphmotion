01 / Buttons

Buttons are weather

Hover changes the air. Press makes it rain. Keyboard focus lights a ring of stars. Every control is a small piece of sky.

Primary

Hover: clouds gather around it. Press: they darken and it rains.

Secondary

Hover: cold mist falls. Press: a breath of fog.

Ghost

Hover: wind lines cross it. Press: a gust.

Icon

Press: one full day passes in seven seconds. Worth pressing twice.

Toggle

On: the sky follows your own clock. Scroll still brings the storm and the stars.

Destructive

Press: lightning strikes the button. Nothing is deleted, it only looks like it.

02 / Pointer and menu

The sky answers you

There is no cursor. There is wind. The sky opens where you move and leaves a trail of light.

Mouse is wind

Clouds part around the pointer and a wake of light follows it. The faster you move, the wider the clearing.

Touch is a gust

Tap anywhere and a ring of wind rolls outward. Drag to carve a path through the clouds.

Menu is a break

Open Product in the bar. The overcast tears open from the button and a shaft of light falls through. Press Esc to close it.

03 / The share moment

Call the storm

Hold the button. Clouds gather while you hold, the air crackles, and the sky holds its breath. Let go and it answers.

04 / Language notes

Five rules of the sky

01

The palette is the clock

Every color is sampled from one sky: gold at the horizon, blue overhead, ink at night. Interface elements never bring a color of their own.

02

Depth is the ramp

 .:-=+*#%@

Thin air is a dot. Thick cloud is an at sign. Density is the only shading, so every picture is made of one alphabet.

03

Hover is temperature, press is rain

Warm things rise, cool things fall. A press releases precipitation: rain, fog, wind or lightning. Each control owns one kind of weather.

04

Weather has inertia

Nothing snaps. A sky eases over two seconds, a button over a third. Reduced motion keeps every still frame and still shows the change.

05

Focus is starlight

Keyboard focus draws a twinkling ring of stars, always paired with a solid outline so it reads on any sky.
