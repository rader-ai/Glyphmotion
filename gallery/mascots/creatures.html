SHOAL mascot, round 2

Meet Nib.

A small cuttlefish who lives in the corner of your site and wears every feeling on its skin. Curious first, shy second, proud about once a day. It carries a tiny lantern on its forehead.

Nib feelsContent

Play directly: click the water to drop a snack, click Nib to poke it, drag it around, or stroke the pointer slowly across its body to pet it. On touch, tap and drag work the same way. Leave it alone and it gets bored, then sleepy, then falls asleep. After 9pm on your clock it gets sleepy faster.

Secret 1: hold your pointer still on its face for three seconds. Secret 2: double-click empty water.

Why this character

A mascot made of the same stuff as the brand.

Most mascots are a face pasted on a logo. Nib is a cuttlefish, the one animal that talks in color. SHOAL talks in colored text on a dark grid. So when Nib changes its mind, the whole body changes: color, pattern, posture, eyes. You can read the mood from across the room.

It speaks in color, so it speaks in our medium.

Every mood has its own skin. Teal and a slow gold ripple for calm. Coral and gold shimmer for delight. Near white for a shock. Water-colored camouflage when shy. Nothing is bolted on: the color text grid that builds the page also builds the feeling.

02

It has eyes, hands and attention.

Big eyes with real W-shaped pupils look at your cursor, your form field, the snack you just dropped. Eight short arms and two long feeding tentacles let it wave, tap, scratch its head and snatch food. A blob cannot do that, and a jellyfish has no face to do it with.

03

It lives in the reef we already built.

Round 1 gave SHOAL a whale, jellies and a lantern. Nib is the resident. Its skin uses only the SHOAL palette (foam, lamp, coral, jelly, kelp) and the little lantern on its forehead is the same lamp color that glows in our logo.

04

It is nobody else's.

Not a blob with eyes, not a robot helper, not a copy of a famous character. A frilled cuttlefish with a forehead lantern that blushes, inks and does a stare contest is specific enough to be ours alone.

Easy to love

People will pet it, feed it, win the stare contest and post the screen recording. Ten clean mood portraits double as a sticker set.

Useful, not cute for its own sake

The same ten moods cover errors, loading, success, empty states and quiet corners. One character, a whole feedback system.

Built to behave

It never covers text, hides on request, sleeps on request, tells screen readers its mood politely and holds calm still poses when motion is off.

A brand that can show a mood is a brand people remember being kind to them.

Character rules

How the brand uses Nib.

  1. Show up where something is happening.Forms, loading, empty states, success, errors, quiet corners. Not as decoration on dense content, and never more than one Nib on screen at a time.
  2. Never block anything.It lives in the corner dock, never over a button, field or paragraph. A hide button is always one click away and a sleep button is right beside it.
  3. React, never interrupt.No popups, no sound, no chat bubbles that open by themselves. It notices, it does not nag. When it does speak, it is one short plain line, like: Hm. That email looks off.
  4. The mood must match the event.Errors are concerned, not tragic. Successes are proud, not wild. It never celebrates a failure and never sulks over a normal click.
  5. Keep the skin in the palette.Colors come from SHOAL: foam, lamp, coral, jelly, kelp and the dark water. Seasonal hats are fine. Logos, ads and sponsor colors on its skin are not.
  6. Respect motion settings.When a visitor asks for less motion, Nib holds calm still poses that still change with its mood. No loops, no sparks, same warmth.
