Meltwork mascot, round two

Meet Fettle.

A small salamander made of slag. Its skin is cooled crust, its cracks are lit from inside, and how it feels is how hot it glows. It walks around your pages, notices what you do, and falls asleep when you do.

  • Personality warm, nosy, easily startled, quietly proud.
  • Try move the pointer near it, drag it by the head, press and hold to scratch its chin, or toss it a coal.
  • Secret type melt anywhere on this page.
Fettle is tucked away.

Why this character

A mood you can read as temperature.

Meltwork turns every interface element into simulated matter, and each material means something. A mascot for that world should not sit on top of the language. It should be made of it. Fettle is made of five materials, and each one carries a job.

"Every project has one question underneath all the others: how hot is this? Fettle answers it before anyone reads a word."

The idea in one line
  1. 01Cracks and spots are emberMomentum. Lit from inside, brighter when work is moving or just landed. Its yellow back spots come from the real fire salamander, redrawn as glowing slag.
  2. 02Gills and breath are smokeWhat passes. Six frills sway behind the head, lift when it is alert and droop when it worries. Sighs leave a puff.
  3. 03The clock is sandIt reads your local time. After 10 pm it is drowsy. After a minute of nothing it curls up and sleeps, and wakes with a yawn.
  4. 04Trouble is steelWhen something fails it cools toward steel blue and its gills droop. It never turns red or angry, and it warms back up when you fix the problem.
  5. 05Words are inkIt says nothing. A question mark, an exclamation point, hearts and a Z are its whole vocabulary.

It can only belong to Meltwork

Fire salamanders come from an old folk story: creatures that live in the flames and walk out unburned. Make one from slag, light its cracks from inside, and you have an animal that only exists in a world of molten matter. It is not a blob, a robot or a paperclip, and it cannot be dropped into another product without looking borrowed.

The silhouette does the recognizing: a big round head with frills, four splayed feet, a long tail with a glowing tip.

It does real jobs in a tracker

First board: it walks the empty canvas and tosses a spark at the first card you add.

Release night: it paces at the front of the progress bar, focused, and holds a proud pose when the deploy lands.

Monday morning: a faint glow beside the board name tells a manager whether the week was warm or stale, with no chart to read.

People will pass it around

It leaves warm footprints that fade. It purrs when you scratch its chin. You can pick it up by the head and swing it. Type "melt" and it collapses into a puddle, then pulls itself back together. Each of those is a ten second clip someone sends to a friend.

And it is cheap: one canvas on the shared loop, paused offscreen, a calm still pose under reduced motion.

Emotion sheet

Nine feelings, all live.

Every tile is a living Fettle, blinking, breathing and watching your pointer. Click or press a tile to make the main character feel it. Each feeling is a set of numbers (eyelids, mouth, gills, posture, heat) that the body springs toward, so transitions overshoot a little and settle, the way a body does.

Character rules

How the brand uses Fettle.

  1. Arrive at the edges of work.

    Fettle appears when a page is empty, waiting or finished: first run, loading, errors, success, the footer. It does not hover over a form someone is in the middle of filling.

  2. Never block, always be dismissible.

    It lives in margins and empty space. Every placement has a Hide button and a Send to sleep button, both reachable by keyboard, and it falls asleep on its own after a minute of nothing.

  3. Never celebrate a failure.

    Errors get concern, not jokes. Fettle may cool and droop, but it does not laugh, shrug or point. Success is the only time it throws a party.

  4. Heat tells the truth.

    It only glows hot when real work is happening or just finished. It never fakes progress, and its mood is never used to push a sale.

  5. Almost silent.

    It speaks in glyphs: a question mark, an exclamation point, hearts, a Z. If it ever uses words they are lowercase, three to eight words and kind: "all saved." "that did not go through. try again?" No puns about the user.

  6. One at a time.

    One Fettle per screen. The emotion sheet is the only crowd. Under reduced motion it holds a calm still pose that still changes with each feeling.
